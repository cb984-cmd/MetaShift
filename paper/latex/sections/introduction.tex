\section{Introduction}
\suppressfloats[t]

A PM$_{2.5}$ monitor can show an abrupt change near a reported Method Code
transition. If nearby monitors move at the same time, the follow-up question is
very different from the question raised by an isolated change. A target-only
detector can identify a numerical break, but it cannot determine whether that
break is local to the target or shared across the comparison network. Forcing a
scope label when the necessary comparison is unavailable can misdirect a
technical investigation just as readily as missing a change.

This is an information-constrained inference problem in computer science:
selective prediction over time-series observations with explicitly limited
channels. The central question is not ``which estimator wins?'' It is:
\begin{quote}
\emph{Given a specified observation channel and an allowed scope-error rate,
what fraction of scope questions can a system answer, and when must it abstain?}
\end{quote}

We call this \emph{information-constrained selective scope auditing}. Here,
\emph{scope} means whether a change is specific to the target or shared by its
declared comparison network. It must be separated from detection and mechanism
(Table~\ref{tab:three-questions}).

\begin{table}[!ht]
\centering
\caption{Three distinct questions raised by a changing monitored time series.}
\label{tab:three-questions}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.18\linewidth}p{0.34\linewidth}X@{}}
\toprule
Question & What it asks & Minimum evidence needed \\
\midrule
Detection & Did the target path change? & Target time series. \\
Scope & Is the change local or shared with the comparison network? & Target
series plus a qualified cross-site comparison. \\
Mechanism & Why did the change occur? & Technical records, domain metadata, and
human review in addition to a comparison. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

A reported AQS Method Code transition is useful as an observable metadata
anchor, but it is not evidence that a device was replaced, failed, or biased.
That distinction makes the real-data audit a test of comparison availability
and principled abstention, rather than a source of unobserved scope or
mechanism labels.

MetaShift-Bench supplies a deliberately target-fixed synthetic test of the
scope question. Every matched pair has exactly identical target observations in
the local and shared arms; only donor participation changes. Consequently, a
target-only policy cannot distinguish balanced local from shared labels in the
stipulated experiment, while a policy receiving target and donor channels may
do so. The benchmark measures the held-out coverage achieved by predeclared
policies at stated answered-case error tolerances.

The primary design extends a binary local-versus-regional endpoint into a
donor-participation continuum. It crosses nominal participation
$q\in\{0,.25,.50,.75,1\}$ with signal strength and adverse comparison
conditions. A bounded structural certificate answers only when
availability-normalized score intervals separate under declared generator
envelopes. A nonpositive margin is retained as a required abstention region,
not hidden inside a weak classifier score.

The report asks five linked research questions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{RQ1:} How much scope-answerability coverage do target-only and
  comparative channels achieve at prespecified error tolerances?
  \item \textbf{RQ2:} When does a structural certificate answer or abstain
  along the partial-scope boundary?
  \item \textbf{RQ3:} How do cross-site and single-series methods compare, and
  does MetaShift have a confidence-supported advantage over standard synthetic
  control?
  \item \textbf{RQ4:} How often can a qualified cross-site audit be formed in
  the AQS deployment inventory, and what dispositions result?
  \item \textbf{RQ5:} What do interval calibration and contextual evidence
  reveal about the limits of real-data auditing?
\end{enumerate}

Our contributions are fourfold:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*.}]
  \item \textbf{Formulate} a target-fixed benchmark that measures selective
  local-versus-shared scope answerability by observation channel.
  \item \textbf{Derive} an availability-normalized partial-scope residual
  identity and a bounded interval-separation condition for structural
  abstention.
  \item \textbf{Evaluate} predeclared confidence and certificate policies on a
  complete adverse-condition grid, retaining failures and identity controls.
  \item \textbf{Audit} an independent AQS deployment inventory to measure
  comparison availability, diagnostic dispositions, and the limits of
  metadata-based interpretation.
\end{enumerate}

The framework builds on established selective classification, information
refinement, change-point detection, and synthetic control. Its contribution is
their bounded joint evaluation for an explicitly target-fixed scope question.
