\section{Results}

\subsection{RQ1: Stable-window synthetic comparison}

\textbf{Answer.} The frozen benchmark distinguishes cross-site methods from
single-series baselines, but no method is uniformly best across all metrics and
paired perturbation families. Table~\ref{tab:synthetic-metrics} reports the
four cross-site methods used for the primary comparison, while
Table~\ref{tab:all-methods} restores all nine frozen methods.
Figure~\ref{fig:synthetic-metrics} visualizes the main cross-site trade-offs
without collapsing them into a post-hoc composite score.

\begin{figure}[tbp]

\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_synthetic_metrics.pdf}
\caption{Held-out stable-regime synthetic metrics for the four cross-site
methods. Each metric is evaluated on the frozen 80-site complete-input-disjoint
partition: lower local-effect MAE and regional false-positive rate are
preferable, whereas higher macro-F1 is preferable. Bars compare different
criteria, not a universal ranking; all numerical values originate in the saved

Standard synthetic control has stronger aggregate attribution ranking and
macro-F1 than both MetaShift variants, while cross-validated MetaShift has the
smallest aggregate local-effect MAE. Nearest-neighbor difference-in-differences
has the lowest regional false-positive rate among these four methods but weaker
effect error and attribution metrics. These are benchmark-specific trade-offs,
not a ranking for every monitoring network.

\input{generated/tables/table_all_methods}

The single-series methods remain in the complete comparison because an anchor
date alone can make a time-series break appear compelling. Their inclusion
shows what is lost when a method does not ask whether target and donors moved
together. N/A local-effect MAE cells are retained rather than replaced by a
surrogate metric: CUSUM, PELT, and rolling-MAD supply change evidence but no
protocol-defined level-effect estimator for the variance-only condition.

The perturbation matrix makes the scope of the aggregate result visible.
Cross-site methods distinguish additive and gradual target-local changes much
better than the single-series baselines under this design, whereas
proportional, temporary, and variance conditions expose different
false-positive and ranking trade-offs. Because each row aggregates a paired
local and regional condition, it tests local-versus-regional discrimination
rather than a generic detection-rate claim.

\subsection{RQ2: Bound on a MetaShift superiority claim}

\textbf{Answer.} Neither MetaShift variant has a confidence-supported aggregate
MAE advantage over standard synthetic control. The paired bootstrap intervals
cross zero for both frozen variants, including cross-validated MetaShift's
\CVPairedMAEDifference{} difference with interval
[\CVPairedMALower{}, \CVPairedMAEUpper{}].

stable overall MetaShift advantage; this negative result closes the
model-superiority route for the frozen benchmark.

\subsection{RQ3: Complete real-anchor audit}

\textbf{Answer.} Only \CompleteComparisons{} of \TotalAnchors{} anchors support
a complete common cross-site comparison under the distinct-physical-donor
rule. The remaining \InsufficientDonorAnchors{} donor-insufficient and
\InputFailureAnchors{} input-window events are retained as applicability
boundaries rather than removed.

\subsection{RQ4: Interval coverage and sensitivity}

\textbf{Answer.} The two available fixed-protocol interval constructions do not
support a generic calibration claim. At nominal 95\%, fixed-weight conditional
block-bootstrap coverage ranges from \ConditionalCoverageLow{} to
\ConditionalCoverageHigh{} over \CoverageInstancesPerMethod{} held-out effect
instances per method. At nominal 90\%, split-conformal coverage ranges from
\ConformalCoverageLow{} to \ConformalCoverageHigh{} and is highly
conservative.

selection-aware synthetic coverage protocol is infeasible within the deadline.
The intervals are therefore reported as conditional diagnostics.

\subsection{RQ5: External context and transfer boundary}

\textbf{Answer.} External evidence supplies limited contextual support, not
ground truth. Of \POCCandidates{} same-site POC candidates,
\POCUsablePairs{} have usable paired hourly windows and
\POCDirectionAgreements{} agree in daily/hourly direction. The targeted
official-document review found \ExternalReviewConfirmations{}/\ExternalReviewEvents{}
dated site-specific confirmations. A missing public notice is unavailable
evidence, not evidence that no physical change occurred.

The independent 88502 pipeline has \SecondaryAnchors{} eligible metadata
anchors and only \SecondaryComplete{} complete common-method comparisons. It
demonstrates separate-pipeline feasibility but is too sparse to support broad
generalization. EPA documentation about network data alignment provides useful
method context \citep{epa_t640_alignment}; it is not a dated site-specific
confirmation for the reviewed anchors.
