\section{Results}

\subsection{RQ0: Scope Answerability Frontier and information gain}

\textbf{Answer.} The frozen v0.5 finite-policy results support the stipulated
target-only impossibility endpoint and show that comparative answerability is
not uniformly available at strict error tolerances. The target-only forced
policy answers every held-out arm (coverage
\VFiveTargetForcedCoveragePercent{}) and has observed conditional scope error
\VFiveTargetForcedRiskPercent{}; accordingly, no positive-coverage target-only
policy qualifies for any reported $\alpha<.5$.
The comparative forced policy also answers every arm (coverage
\VFiveComparativeForcedCoveragePercent{}) but has observed error
\VFiveComparativeForcedRiskPercent{}. Its confidence-selective policies
calibrated at $.01$, $.05$, and $.10$ answer
\VFiveStrictConfidenceAnsweredArms{} evaluation arms. Only the policy
calibrated at $.20$ reaches a positive comparative envelope: coverage
\VFiveComparativeFrontierTwentyPercent{} at observed error
\VFiveComparativeRiskTwentyPercent{}.

\input{generated/tables/table_v05_frontier}

At $\alpha=.20$, the achieved finite-policy Scope Answerability Gain from the
target-only to comparative channel is therefore
\VFiveComparativeFrontierTwentyPercent{}. Its 95\% component-bootstrap interval
is $[\VFiveComparativeGainLowerPercent{},\VFiveComparativeGainUpperPercent{}]$.
At $.01$, $.05$, and $.10$, both finite-policy frontiers are zero. These values
compare frozen policies on held-out synthetic components; they do not estimate
a universal optimum or guarantee future conditional scope risk.

\subsection{RQ0a: Structural certificate and retained failure boundary}

\textbf{Answer.} The bounded certificate is internally valid within its
declared synthetic contract, but it is intentionally incomplete. It answered
\VFiveCertificateAnsweredPairs{} of \VFiveEvaluationPairs{} matched pairs
(\VFiveCertificateCoveragePercent{}), with
\VFiveCertificateObservedErrorDisplay{} among
\VFiveCertificateAnsweredArms{} answered scope arms. It recovered
\VFiveCertificateEfficiencyPercent{} of the pairs deemed answerable by the
predeclared simulation-information oracle and abstained on the remaining
\VFiveNonpositiveMarginPairs{} pairs
(\VFiveCertificateAbstentionPercent{}), including all \VFiveQZeroPairs{}
observationally indistinguishable $q=0$ negative controls. It has
\VFiveEnvelopeViolations{} observed envelope violations. These finite-grid
observations hold only under the bounded generator; they are neither evidence
of zero population risk nor a deployable real-network certificate.

\input{generated/tables/table_v05_certificate}

The $q=0$ stratum contains \VFiveQZeroPairs{} matched pairs with exactly
identical target and donor observations in both scope arms. It produces
\VFiveQZeroCertificateAnsweredPairs{} certificate answers and no
oracle-answerable pair, as required by the negative-control contract. For
$q>0$, the certificate coverage rises with nominal participation but decreases
under contamination, nonlinear raw fields, and heteroskedastic noise. The
result does not imply that all real partial scope is objectively ambiguous; it
shows where this particular structural bound refuses to answer.

\subsection{Historical v0.4 endpoint sanity check}

The immutable v0.4 construction supplies only an endpoint check: when matched
local and regional worlds hold the target sequence fixed, target-only
classification has observed error $0.5$, while its constructed comparative
rule has zero observed error on that finite endpoint evaluation. It does not
measure a risk--coverage frontier, calibration-selected abstention, partial
scope, a failure boundary, or real measurement mechanisms. The v0.5
continuum is therefore the primary scope evidence; v0.4 is retained as an
historical sanity check and is not rerun or reinterpreted.

\subsection{RQ1: Stable-window synthetic comparison}

\textbf{Historical answer.} The frozen v0.3.2 benchmark distinguishes cross-site methods from
single-series baselines, but no method is uniformly best across all metrics and
paired perturbation families. Table~\ref{tab:synthetic-metrics} reports the
four cross-site methods used for the primary comparison, while
Table~\ref{tab:all-methods} restores all nine frozen methods.
Figure~\ref{fig:synthetic-metrics} visualizes the main cross-site trade-offs
without collapsing them into a post-hoc composite score.

Standard synthetic control has stronger aggregate attribution ranking and

not a ranking for every monitoring network.

The single-series methods remain in the complete comparison because an anchor

together. N/A local-effect MAE cells are retained rather than replaced by a
surrogate metric: CUSUM, PELT, and rolling-MAD supply change evidence but no

\subsection{RQ4: Interval coverage and sensitivity}

\textbf{Answer.} The two available fixed-protocol interval constructions do not
support a generic calibration claim. At nominal 95\%, fixed-weight conditional
block-bootstrap coverage ranges from \ConditionalCoverageLow{} to
\ConditionalCoverageHigh{} over \CoverageInstancesPerMethod{} held-out effect
instances per method. At nominal 90\%, split-conformal coverage ranges from
\ConformalCoverageLow{} to \ConformalCoverageHigh{} and is highly
conservative.

\subsection{RQ5: External context and transfer boundary}

\textbf{Answer.} External evidence supplies limited contextual support, not
ground truth. Of \POCCandidates{} same-site POC candidates,
\POCUsablePairs{} have usable paired hourly windows and
\POCDirectionAgreements{} agree in daily/hourly direction. The targeted
official-document review found \ExternalReviewConfirmations{}/\ExternalReviewEvents{}
dated site-specific confirmations. A missing public notice is unavailable
evidence, not evidence that no physical change occurred.

The separately pinned QA-collocation review contains 12 candidate records, of
which 2 match the target POC and 0 provide adequate matched pre/post windows.
It is therefore an evidence-availability result, not a validation of a
measurement-bias hypothesis.

The independent 88502 pipeline has \SecondaryAnchors{} eligible metadata
anchors and only \SecondaryComplete{} complete common-method comparisons. It
demonstrates separate-pipeline feasibility but is too sparse to support broad
generalization. EPA documentation about network data alignment provides useful
method context \citep{epa_t640_alignment}; it is not a dated site-specific
confirmation for the reviewed anchors.
