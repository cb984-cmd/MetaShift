\section*{Abstract}
\noindent When a monitored time series changes, a target-only detector can
identify the break but cannot determine whether it is local to that target or
shared with a comparison network. This distinction matters because a local
anomaly and a regional common shock call for different follow-up actions, while
many real monitoring records lack reliable scope labels. We formulate selective
scope auditing as an information-constrained problem: at a stated error
tolerance, which cases can an observation channel answer, and when should it
abstain?

\noindent MetaShift-Bench holds the target path exactly fixed across paired
local and shared synthetic worlds and varies only donor-side participation. Its
primary target-fixed experiment contains \VFiveEvaluationComponents{} held-out
components, \VFiveEvaluationPairs{} matched pairs, and a complete
$5\times4\times2^5$ adverse-condition grid. In the finite predeclared policy
set, target-only information answers no held-out cases below 50\% tolerated
error. Comparative information also answers none through $\alpha=.10$, then
reaches \VFiveComparativeFrontierTwentyPercent{} coverage at $\alpha=.20$ with
\VFiveComparativeRiskTwentyPercent{} observed conditional error. A structural
interval-separation certificate answered \VFiveCertificateAnsweredPairs{} of
\VFiveEvaluationPairs{} matched pairs (\VFiveCertificateCoveragePercent{}),
with no observed errors among \VFiveCertificateAnsweredArms{} answered scope
arms. It recovered \VFiveCertificateEfficiencyPercent{} of the pairs deemed
answerable by the predeclared simulation-information oracle and abstained on
the remaining \VFiveNonpositiveMarginPairs{} pairs
(\VFiveCertificateAbstentionPercent{}), including all \VFiveQZeroPairs{}
observationally indistinguishable $q=0$ negative controls. These finite-grid
results hold only under the bounded synthetic generator and do not constitute
a population-risk guarantee or a deployable real-network certificate.

\noindent We prove the target-only impossibility statement under matched,
balanced target laws, derive the availability-normalized partial-scope residual
gap, and give a sufficient condition for structural abstention. A separate AQS
deployment audit measures comparison availability and abstention in real public
records; it supplies neither scope labels nor mechanism truth. In its
independent stable-regime benchmark, cross-site methods substantially exceed
single-series baselines in local-versus-shared ranking, but MetaShift shows no
confidence-supported aggregate advantage over standard synthetic control. The
central result is therefore an evaluation framework for answering only
information-supported scope questions.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} local-versus-shared change; time-series auditing;
selective prediction; cross-site comparison; structural abstention.
