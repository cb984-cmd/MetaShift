\section*{Abstract}
Regulatory monitoring records can exhibit discontinuities caused by changing
environmental conditions, data processing, or a reported measurement method.
An isolated time series cannot distinguish a station-specific discontinuity from
a regional pattern. This report presents MetaShift-Bench, a reproducible
metadata-anchored audit benchmark for reported United States Environmental
Protection Agency (EPA) Air Quality System (AQS) PM2.5 Method Code
transitions. A Method Code transition is treated as a reproducible event anchor,
not as proof of physical instrument replacement, fault, or causal measurement
bias. The protocol constructs pre-transition geographically constrained
counterfactuals, compares cross-site and single-series methods under known
synthetic perturbations, and preserves every eligible real event with its
diagnostics or explicit failure reason.

The frozen evidence inventory contains \CanonicalRecords{} canonical daily
observations from \MonitorSeries{} monitor time series and \TotalAnchors{}
persistent anchors. Only \ThreeDonorAnchors{} anchors retain at least three
distinct physical donors; \CompleteComparisons{} receive complete common-method
comparisons, while \InsufficientDonorAnchors{} are donor-insufficient and
\InputFailureAnchors{} have estimator input-window failures. The
stable-regime benchmark uses \SyntheticCases{} physical target sites split into
\CalibrationCases{} calibration and \EvaluationCases{} evaluation sites with
disjoint complete target-plus-donor footprints. Standard synthetic control has
aggregate local-effect MAE \StdSCMAE{}, AUPRC \StdSCAUPRC{}, and macro-F1
\StdSCFOne{}. Cross-validated MetaShift has lower point-estimate MAE
(\CVMAE{}) but its paired difference versus standard synthetic control is
\CVPairedMAEDifference{} (95\% CI [\CVPairedMALower{},
\CVPairedMAEUpper{}]). Thus the evidence does not support a general MetaShift
superiority claim. The contribution is instead a transparent benchmark,
complete event audit, layered diagnostics, and a reproducible account of
applicability limits.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} air-quality monitoring; PM2.5; metadata anchors;
synthetic control; change-point attribution; reproducible audit.
