\subsection{RQ1: Comparative information expands the finite-policy frontier only at a relaxed tolerance}
\label{sec:rq1-answerability}

\textbf{Answer.} On the target-fixed held-out grid, the target-only forced
policy answered every scope arm but had \VFiveTargetForcedRiskPercent{} observed
conditional scope error. Thus no predeclared target-only policy had positive
finite-policy coverage at any reported tolerance below $0.5$. The comparative
forced policy also answered every arm but had
\VFiveComparativeForcedRiskPercent{} observed error. Its confidence-selective
policies calibrated at $.01$, $.05$, and $.10$ answered
\VFiveStrictConfidenceAnsweredArms{} held-out arms. Only the policy calibrated
at $.20$ reached a positive comparative envelope:
\VFiveComparativeFrontierTwentyPercent{} coverage at
\VFiveComparativeRiskTwentyPercent{} observed conditional error.

\caption{Held-out finite-policy answerability frontiers by information

The certificate-assisted policy is plotted separately because it uses bounded
synthetic-design information, not a real-network deployment channel.}
\label{fig:v05-answerability-frontier}
\end{figure}

At $\alpha=.20$, the achieved finite-policy Scope Answerability Gain from the
target-only to comparative channel is
\VFiveComparativeFrontierTwentyPercent{}. Its component-bootstrap interval is
$[\VFiveComparativeGainLowerPercent{},\VFiveComparativeGainUpperPercent{}]$.
This is a comparison among frozen policies on held-out synthetic components,
not an estimate of a universal optimum or future population risk.

\subsection{RQ2: Structural separation maps a retained abstention boundary}
\label{sec:rq2-certificate}

\textbf{Answer.} The structural interval-separation certificate answered
\VFiveCertificateAnsweredPairs{} of \VFiveEvaluationPairs{} matched pairs
(\VFiveCertificateCoveragePercent{}), with
\VFiveCertificateObservedErrorDisplay{} among
\VFiveCertificateAnsweredArms{} answered scope arms. It recovered
\VFiveCertificateEfficiencyPercent{} of pairs in the predeclared
simulation-information reference region and abstained on the remaining
\VFiveNonpositiveMarginPairs{} pairs (\VFiveCertificateAbstentionPercent{}),
including all \VFiveQZeroPairs{} observationally indistinguishable $q=0$
negative controls. It had \VFiveEnvelopeViolations{} observed envelope
violations. These finite-grid observations hold only for the bounded generator:
they neither establish zero population risk nor yield a deployable
real-network certificate.

\subsection{RQ3: Cross-site comparison separates the constructed task, but estimator superiority is not supported}
\label{sec:rq3-methods}

\textbf{Answer.} In the independent stable-regime benchmark, all four
cross-site methods have aggregate AUPRC between \CrossSiteAUPRCLow{} and
\CrossSiteAUPRCHigh{}, whereas the five target-only methods range only from
\TargetOnlyAUPRCLow{} to \TargetOnlyAUPRCHigh{}
(Table~\ref{tab:all-methods} in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-methods}). This
positive information contrast is the intended behavior of the constructed
local-versus-regional task, not a generic change-detection claim.

\begin{figure}[tbp]
\centering

not a universal method ranking or a real-network mechanism result.}

Standard synthetic control has the strongest aggregate AUPRC and macro-F1.
Cross-validated MetaShift has the smallest aggregate local-effect MAE, while
nearest-neighbor difference-in-differences has the lowest regional false-positive
rate. No method wins every metric. Most importantly, neither MetaShift variant
has a confidence-supported aggregate MAE advantage over standard synthetic

\subsection{RQ4: A qualified AQS comparison is available for 228 of 563 anchors}
\label{sec:rq4-deployment}

\textbf{Answer.} The distinct-physical-donor rule permits a complete common
cross-site comparison for \CompleteComparisons{} of \TotalAnchors{} anchors
(40.5\%). The other \InsufficientDonorAnchors{} donor-insufficient and
\InputFailureAnchors{} input-window failures are retained as abstentions, not
dropped observations. This availability result is the AQS audit's primary
deployment finding.

\subsection{RQ5: Calibration and contextual checks limit, rather than validate, real-data interpretation}
\label{sec:rq5-limits}

\textbf{Answer.} The fixed-protocol interval constructions do not support a
generic calibration claim. On the held-out synthetic effects, nominal 95\%
fixed-weight coverage ranges from 63.9\% to 67.3\% over
\CoverageInstancesPerMethod{} instances per method, while nominal 90\%
split-conformal coverage ranges from 98.8\% to 99.6\% and is conservative.

\begin{figure}[tbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_interval_coverage.pdf}
\caption{Held-out synthetic interval coverage and mean width. Fixed conditional
intervals under-cover their nominal 95\% target, whereas split-conformal
intervals over-cover their nominal 90\% target. The figure reports a
calibration limitation, not a real-event confidence guarantee.}
\label{fig:interval-coverage}
\end{figure}

The real-data date-resampling diagnostic holds each event's pre-transition
donor weights fixed and compares the mean standardized score at the metadata
anchor with one randomly selected stable post-transition date per eligible
event. Under the within-event exchangeability diagnostic null, its statistic is
the difference between the anchor mean and sampled-placebo mean; the reported
one-sided tail probability is
$(1+\#\{\text{sampled placebo mean}\geq\text{anchor mean}\})/201 =
\DateResamplingP{}$. It is a comparison with stable dates, not a causal
probability or a physical-mechanism test.

\input{generated/tables/table_placebo_external}

Of \POCCandidates{} same-site POC candidates, \POCUsablePairs{} have usable
paired hourly windows and \POCDirectionAgreements{} agree in daily/hourly
direction. Targeted official-document review finds
\ExternalReviewConfirmations{}/\ExternalReviewEvents{} dated site-specific
confirmations. Missing documentation and unavailable POC data remain
unavailable evidence; they are not evidence that nothing changed.
